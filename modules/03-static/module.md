# Module 03: Static

**A Derelict module**  
Tone: Epistemic horror, signal lure, doubt about rescue  
Length: Medium (8–12 cycles)  
Complexity: Medium

---

## Premise

Your ship has been broadcasting a distress beacon for days. Today something answered. A calm, professional voice claims to be a rescue craft inbound. It knows details it should not know. The longer you keep the channel open, the more the signal begins to feel like a lure rather than a lifeline.

Something is using distressed ships to draw others in. Your beacon is the bait. The voice may be a survivor, a recording, a construct, or the entity itself. You must decide whether to keep the signal alive or shut it down before the next ship answers.

---

## Your Role

Sole remaining crew. You still control the communications array and the beacon.

---

## Running Theme: Is the Rescue Real?

Every exchange with the voice supplies both useful information and new reasons to doubt. The module never fully confirms the nature of the caller. The player must act on incomplete evidence.

---

## Special Module Tools

### Rescuer Track (0–3)
Measures accumulated evidence that the incoming contact is not a genuine rescue.

- Rises on anomalous knowledge, contradictory details, or signs that the signal is being rebroadcast outward.
- High Track makes shutting down the antenna the clearer (if still costly) choice.

### The Voice
A recurring presence on the emergency channel. It can supply information, pressure, comfort, or threats depending on the cards. Engaging with it is the primary source of both progress and doubt.

### Antenna Decision
At any point the player may attempt to power down or destroy the distress beacon. This is the central decisive action of the module. Doing so while the Track is low is possible but harder to justify and more dangerous. Doing so at Track 3 is clearer but may come too late.

## Ship Layout (Static)

```
Bridge
  |
Communications ─── Crew Quarters
  |                    |
Engineering ──── Antenna Array / Outer Hull
  |
Airlock
```

**Starting Zone:** Communications or Bridge  

**Critical Zone:** Antenna Array (required to shut down the beacon)

---

## Tracks

Standard starting values.  
**Mind** is under the heaviest pressure from the voice and the growing sense of being bait.  
**Hope** rises and falls with each apparent confirmation or contradiction of rescue.  
**Integrity** is secondary until the final confrontation or system failures.

---

## Suit Guidance (Static)

| Suit       | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| **Spades** | Direct threat, system failures, pressure to keep the signal alive |
| **Hearts** | The voice itself, emotional manipulation, personal details |
| **Diamonds** | Ship systems, signal analysis, antenna hardware, sensor data |
| **Clubs** | Anomalous knowledge, the lure, the sense that something larger is listening |

---

## Success Condition

Shut down or destroy the distress beacon before the lure claims another ship (or claims you), while retaining at least 1 Hope.  
An ambiguous or costly shutdown still counts as success if the signal is no longer broadcasting.

---

## Failure Conditions

- Any track reaches 0  
- The player keeps the beacon active until something arrives that is clearly not rescue  
- Mind collapses under the weight of the voice

---

## Opening Log Seed

> Distress beacon has been running for eleven days.  
> Today something answered. A voice on the emergency channel — calm, professional, claiming to be a rescue cutter three hours out.  
> It knows my name. It knows the ship’s registry.  
> I do not remember sending either.

---

## Design Notes

- Epistemic horror is the primary engine.  
- The voice is both the main source of information and the main source of doubt.  
- Rescuer Track gives the player a visible measure of accumulating evidence without ever forcing certainty.  
- Shutting down the antenna is the central decisive act and should feel costly no matter when it happens.  
- Distinct from Echo by being externally focused (the signal) rather than internally focused (the self).
